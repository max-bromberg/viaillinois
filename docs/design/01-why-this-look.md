# 01 Why this look

## Who the site is for

VIA is read by two kinds of people. Students in the Department of Electrical and Computer
Engineering at the University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign open it to find out what is
happening this week, usually on a phone, often between classes, sometimes on the lobby
screen in the Electrical and Computer Engineering Building as they walk past. Board members
of the department's student organizations open it to put their events in front of those
students and to keep their own schedules straight.

Both groups are busy, both are technical, and both have seen a great many websites that
look the same. A page that looks generated is a page they will not come back to on purpose.
A page that feels like it was made in the building, for them, is one they will send to a
friend.

## What was wrong before

The first version of the site was built quickly during a course, on a stock component kit,
and it looked like it. Reviewing it in September 2026 the problems were specific:

- The neutrals were a stock cool gray that had nothing to do with the teal in the mark, so
  the brand color sat on the page like a sticker.
- The primary teal used on buttons and links failed the contrast threshold for text.
- The typeface was whatever the device had. The page had no voice and its numbers did not
  align.
- Icons were emoji, which draw differently on every platform.
- The feed was a grid of identical bordered cards. To learn when anything was happening you
  had to read every card, which is the opposite of an agenda.
- The calendar used colors from outside the palette.
- The circuit board drawn behind the page was too faint to register, so the one distinctive
  idea the site had was invisible.

Two further passes warmed that design without changing it, and each still carried the marks
of a template: a small label above every heading, a stripe on every card, a pill on every
status, a tile around every number. The team named those marks directly and asked for a
site that students would recognize as bespoke.

## What the redesign set out to do

1. Keep the mark exactly as it is.
2. Keep the palette that revision 1 established, since it was already right.
3. Remove every habit of the template, and replace each with something the site can own.
4. Make the page feel warm and personal without making it harder to use.
5. Give future work a system, so that the next screen looks like it belongs.

## Where the look came from

Three facts about VIA supplied everything.

**It is an agenda.** The name says so. Time is the primary axis of the site, which is why
day names and times are the largest things on the feed and why the page opens with the
current hour.

**It lives in a building with a lobby screen.** The kiosk is seen from across a room. The
condensed display face, the poster sized titles and the big numerals began as a kiosk
requirement and turned out to be the voice of the whole site.

**Its mark is made of 45 degree strokes on a circuit board.** The cut corner, the pad and the
trace are all taken from the logo. Nothing decorative on the site was invented; it was
found in the mark.

The rest follows from those three. The sky came from asking what the first question of an
agenda is, which is what is on right now, and answering it with color before a single event
is read. Color as light came from refusing stripes and pills while still needing to tell
organizations and statuses apart. The highlighter came from the way a student marks a
printed schedule.
