# 03 The look

This document describes the identity in one place. The numbers behind each part are in the
documents that follow.

## The mark

The mark is unchanged: three angled strokes in teal `#00aaaf`, with rounded ends, the middle
one reading as a V, an I and an A at once. Its 45 degree geometry supplies every shape the
site draws. The mark is always shown in its own teal on paper or on a sky, and always in
white on the kiosk and the night sky. It is never recolored, outlined, rotated or placed
inside a container.

The full name, Virtually Integrated Agenda, sits beside the mark in the navigation in the
condensed display face at 600, and reveals itself on hover the way it does today.

## The sky

The top band of every page is a gradient of the campus sky at the current hour. There are
four skies (morning, afternoon, dusk, night) in each theme, blended into each other at the
edges of their hours. The band holds the navigation, the greeting and the clock, and it ends
in a cut at its bottom left corner. Everything below it is paper. On the kiosk the sky is
the whole screen.

The sky drifts a few pixels a minute. It holds still under reduced motion.

## The voice

Bricolage Grotesque is the voice. It has a width axis from 75 to 100 percent, a weight axis
from 200 to 800 and an optical size axis from 12 to 96. The site uses it in three voices:

- **Condensed 800** for headlines, day names, times, course codes and counts. Anything meant
  to be read from across a room.
- **Wide 300** for the sentence that sits beside a headline: greetings, ledes, the line
  under a title.
- **Condensed 200** for clocks, at the largest sizes.

Width 85 to 90 at 700 carries buttons, navigation, event titles in the feed and the headings
in the filter rail. IBM Plex Sans carries paragraphs. IBM Plex Mono carries rooms, course
codes, dates in the small, and every string a person copies.

## Color as light

The palette is a teal scale with the logo at its center, a teal cast on every neutral in
both themes, Illinois orange as the one warm accent, and a set of eight matched hues for
tags and for the calendar. It arrives on the page in three ways.

- **The lamp.** An organization's color falls across its row from the top left corner and
  fades out by a third of the way across. On hover it brightens. On the event page it
  lights the whole top of the poster.
- **The highlighter.** Tags and statuses are words with a stroke of color under their lower
  half, skewed 10 degrees and rotated a little over one degree, the way a marker lands. An
  unselected tag is the word over a dotted line.
- **The glow.** Whatever is happening now gets a slow pulse around its pad and a warm lamp
  on its row. Today's name in the agenda and the count of tonight's events are orange.

## The cut

One corner of a shape is cut at 45 degrees. It appears on the top right of the primary and
outlined buttons, the toast and the event row, on the sky tiles, and on the bottom left of
the sky band and the masthead. An outlined shape carries its border around the cut at full
width, because the border and the fill are two chamfered layers rather than one clipped
box.

## The pad

A small square rotated 45 degrees, with a slight radius on its corners. It has three states:
filled (on, or an organization's mark), hollow (off), and lit (a soft halo, meaning live).
It is the checkbox, the organization mark, the thumb of the switch, the needle of the theme
dial, the marker at the start of a field, the marker under the active page in the
navigation, and the marker beside each day in the agenda.

## The trace

A line that turns at 45 degrees and ends in a pad. It decorates the masthead, the sky band,
the sky tiles and the empty state, and it is the board that lights up on the kiosk. It is
drawn thinly in teal, with one pad in orange where a signal is live. It is the only
ornament the system permits, because it is also the mark's own geometry.

## The numerals

Counts, times and course codes are set as large condensed numerals followed by their
meaning in words: "2 tonight in ECEB", "12 this week", "9 days to the ECE 210 midterm". A
number and its meaning stay one phrase. There is never a tile with a number in it.

## What the look is not

For reviewers, the tells that a screen has drifted back toward a template:

- A small uppercase label above a heading.
- A bar or stripe of color along the edge of a card.
- A filled pill for a status or a tag.
- A number inside a bordered tile with a caption under it.
- A grid of identical bordered cards where a list would do.
- A rounded rectangle with a one pixel border used as the default container.
- A shadow on something that does not float.
- A gradient anywhere except the sky, the primary button, the lamp, the kiosk board and the
  underline under the word a rail or a listing is currently sorted or filtered by.
- Emoji standing in for icons.
