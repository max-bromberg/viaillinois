# 02 Philosophy

Eight commitments. A new screen is checked against every one of them. If a screen breaks
one, the screen changes, not the commitment. Each is written with the reason behind it and
the test that tells you whether a screen keeps it.

## 1. The page is an agenda

Time is the primary axis. Events read top to bottom, grouped by day, with the time set as
the largest thing in each row, so a week can be scanned without reading a single title.

*Reason.* The site exists to answer "what is on, and when". A layout that makes a person
open each item to learn when it happens has failed at the one job.

*Test.* Cover the titles with your hand. Can you still tell what is happening tonight, and
when?

## 2. It greets you

Every arrival gets a sentence written for the moment: the time of day, what is on tonight,
how far the next midterm is. Signed in, it uses the person's first name and counts only the
organizations they follow.

*Reason.* A student opening the site between classes should feel that the page knows what
time it is and where they are. That is what makes a tool feel personal rather than
generated.

*Test.* Does the first sentence on the page change if you open it at a different hour?

## 3. The sky sets the mood

The top band of every page is a gradient of the sky over the Electrical and Computer
Engineering Building at the current campus hour: morning gold, afternoon teal, dusk peach,
night. It drifts slowly. It is the one gradient that carries meaning, and it is different
every time you come back.

*Reason.* Answering the agenda's first question with color, before any text is read, and
making a page opened at 8 in the morning feel different from one opened after lab at 9 at
night.

*Test.* Is the sky confined to the top band, with paper below it? Does the page hold its
contrast at every hour in both themes?

## 4. Teal is the identity

Teal marks VIA itself: the mark, the primary action on a screen, links, the active page,
and the on state of a control. It is never used to mean a status.

*Reason.* A color that means both "this is us" and "this is confirmed" means neither.

*Test.* Is every teal thing on the screen either the site or an action of the site?

## 5. Orange is the moment

Illinois orange is spent on what is happening now or next: the count of tonight's events,
today's name in the agenda, the pad of the live event, the warm end of the dusk sky. Nothing
else may use it.

*Reason.* Orange is the complement of the teal and the color of the campus. Spent
everywhere it is noise. Spent only on now, it is a signal you learn to look for.

*Test.* Count the orange things on the screen. Are all of them about now?

## 6. Color is light, not paint

Color arrives from somewhere. An organization's color is a lamp lighting the corner of its
row. A tag or status is a word with a highlighter stroke under it. What is live has a glow
around its pad. There are no color bars, no stripes, no filled pills, and no tile with a
number in it.

*Reason.* Bars and pills are the signature of every template. Light is how the world shows
color, and it lets the same color be gentle on a row and strong on a poster without
changing hue.

*Test.* Point at each colored thing. Can you say where the light is coming from?

## 7. One typeface does the talking

Bricolage Grotesque, across its whole width and weight range, is the voice of the site.
Condensed extra bold for what must be read from across a room. Wide light for the sentence
beside it. Thin condensed for clocks. IBM Plex Sans carries paragraphs and IBM Plex Mono
carries rooms and course codes. There are no eyebrow labels above headings.

*Reason.* A page that reads like a poster pinned up in the atrium is a page students will
recognize as theirs. A single family pushed to its extremes gives more voice than three
families used politely.

*Test.* Is every heading, day name, time and count set in the display face? Is there any
small uppercase label sitting above a heading?

## 8. Motion has a reason

Five movements, each tied to a fact about the page: the now pad breathes, the sky drifts,
rows settle into place, a lamp brightens under the cursor, the theme dial slides. Nothing
else moves, and all of it stops for anyone whose system asks for reduced motion.

*Reason.* Motion that tells you something is welcome. Motion that decorates is the fastest
way to make a page feel generated.

*Test.* For each thing that moves, what fact is it telling you?
