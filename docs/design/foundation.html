Good evening, Illinois.

Revision 3 leaves the template behind. The page takes its color from the sky over campus at this hour, its voice from one typeface pushed to its condensed and wide extremes, and its shapes from the 45 degree cut in the mark. Nothing on it is a card with a stripe.

What changed since revision 3

A refinement pass. One of the fixes is larger than it sounds: the typefaces on the previous revisions were never actually loading.

The real typefaces, for the first timeThe font files inlined in revisions 1 to 3 were the wrong subsets and held almost none of the alphabet, so the page fell back to a system font for every letter except the a. That is what the odd a was. This revision carries the correct files. Everything you see is Bricolage Grotesque and IBM Plex for the first time.
The cut has a true borderAn outlined button is now two chamfered layers, a border layer and a fill layer inset by the border width along every edge including the diagonal. The cut reads as a corner, never as a gap.
Focus follows the cutKeyboard focus on a cut button thickens the border layer to three pixels in teal, so the ring has the same shape as the button. Every other control gets a two pixel teal ring set four pixels out.
Contrast, checked and adjustedHighlighter text now mixes 62 percent hue with ink and clears 4.8 to 1 on its own stroke in both themes. Organization names mix 50 percent with ink. Nothing on the page is set smaller than 12 pixels, and the faint gray is gone from running text.
Targets a hand can hitEvery pad control sits inside a 32 pixel target, and tags in the rail have the same. The ink of the pad stays 12 pixels.
Three questions held for youListed under Accessibility below. None of them are made here, because each would change the look.

The sky over ECEB

The site knows the campus hour already; it uses it to decide which events are upcoming. Now it also uses it to paint the top of the page. Four skies, blended between at the edges of each, drifting a few pixels a minute. Signed in at midnight the page is dark whether or not you chose the dark theme, because that is what the window looks like.

Morning6:00 to 11:00
Afternoon11:00 to 17:00
Dusk17:00 to 21:00

Why this is not decoration. The feed's first question is always the same one: what is on right now. Painting the hour onto the page answers it before a single event is read, and it makes a page you open at 8 in the morning feel different from the one you open after lab at 9 at night.

How it stays usable. The sky lives only in the top band. Everything below it is paper. Text on the sky uses the same ink as the page, and the night sky switches to light ink. Every sky is checked for contrast against both inks.

One typeface, three voices

Bricolage Grotesque has a width axis from 75 to 100 percent and a weight axis from 200 to 800. Used at its extremes it stops looking like a font choice and starts looking like a poster somebody pinned up in the ECEB atrium. Plex Sans still carries paragraphs and Plex Mono still carries rooms and course codes.

Counts are set as words with a large numeral, never as a tile with a number in it. The number and its meaning stay one phrase.

Color is light, not paint

Revision 2 still put color on things: a bar along the top of a principle, a fill behind a status. Now color comes from somewhere. It falls across a surface from a corner, it sits under a word like a highlighter, it glows around a pad. The palette is unchanged; how it lands is not.

The lamp

An organization's color lights the top left corner of its row and fades out by a third of the way across. On hover it brightens. On the event page it lights the whole top of the poster.

The highlighter

Tags and statuses are words with a stroke of color under them, slightly off square, the way a marker lands. An unselected tag is dotted. No pills anywhere on the site.

The glow

What is happening now gets a pulse around its pad and a warm lamp on its row. Today's name in the agenda is orange. That is the entire budget for orange on a page, spent where it counts.

Parts nobody else has

Every control below is ours. The shapes come from the mark, the type from the voice above, and the color from the lamp and the highlighter. None of them is a rounded rectangle with a border.

Buttons

The primary is cut at the top right corner and filled with the Current gradient. An outlined button is a border layer and a fill layer, both cut, so the border holds its width around the corner. The third button shows keyboard focus: the border layer thickens to three pixels of teal. Danger stays outlined until confirmed.

Motion with a reason

Five movements, each tied to a fact about the page. Everything else holds still. All of it stops for anyone whose system asks for reduced motion.

The now pad breathesA slow pulse, once every 1.6 seconds, on the pad of whatever is happening right now.
The sky driftsThe gradient's stops move a few pixels a minute, so the band is never quite the same twice.
Rows settleOn load each row eases up eight pixels into place, sixty milliseconds after the one above it. Never from invisible.
The lamp brightensReaching for a row with the cursor raises its organization's lamp from fourteen to thirty percent over 200 milliseconds.
The dial turnsChanging theme rotates the pad along the dial and crossfades the sky over 400 milliseconds, rather than snapping.

Built in

  • Every control has a visible focus state. On a cut button the ring is the border layer itself, so it keeps the shape.
  • Pads carry meaning by shape, filled against hollow, never by color alone. The lamp is always beside the organization name. The heat ribbon prints the count on every week.
  • Every pad control, tag and organization mark sits inside a 32 pixel target.
  • Nothing is set below 12 pixels. The faint gray is used for hairlines only, never for words.
  • All five movements stop under prefers reduced motion, including the drifting sky.
  • Day names are headings, times carry machine readable datetimes, and the greeting is a live region that updates once when the counts arrive.
  • Text on the sky uses the page ink; the night sky switches the band to light ink. Every sky is measured against both.
Three decisions for the team.
  • Organization colors are chosen by each board and cannot be guaranteed. Either the platform clamps a chosen color to a lightness that clears 4.5 to 1 when mixed, or the name is set in ink and only the pad and lamp carry the color.
  • The highlighter is a stroke under the lower half of a word. If a reviewer wants the full text over a solid background, the stroke would become a full height tint at 18 percent, which is a quieter look.
  • A person who has explicitly chosen the light theme still gets a dark band at night. That respects the window, not the preference. The alternative is a dusk band all night for explicit light theme users.

Building it without a kit

The client has three stock components today. This revision needs none of them. Everything is a Svelte 5 component under the shared component directory, styled from tokens, with a test that renders it in both themes.

New primitives

  • Pad, with filled, hollow and lit states. Used by Checkbox, OrganizationMark, Switch, Dial and Field.
  • Cut, a wrapper that applies the chamfer at a given size and corner.
  • Lamp, which paints an organization's color from a corner at a given strength.
  • Highlight, the marker stroke under a word, on or off.
  • Sky, which reads the campus hour and paints the band, with drift and reduced motion handled inside.
  • Numeral, for large condensed numbers with a small unit.

Composed parts

  • Button (primary, secondary, quiet, danger), Field, Toast, EmptyState
  • SkyBand with Nav, Greeting and Clock
  • DayGroup and EventRow for the agenda
  • Poster for the event page
  • TermRibbon and ExamRow for midterms
  • KioskStage and KioskRail

Bits UI stays as the headless layer for menus, dialogs and the date picker. Tailwind stays for spacing and layout, with the tokens renamed. Bricolage Grotesque ships as one 54 KB variable file with both axes.
